For a reliable result

  • Use daylight. Avoid flash and hard shadows across the leaf.
  • Get close. Fill the frame with one leaf or a small group of plants.
  • Keep it sharp. Hold the phone steady so the leaf veins are clear.
  • One crop per photo. Do not mix rice and maize in the same picture.

The result is a screening aid. A soil test or a visit from an agriculture officer gives the final answer.

        How accurate is it?

        These figures come from images the models never saw during training. Small datasets give rough estimates, so treat them as a guide.

        CheckImages usedMeasured accuracyWorth knowing
        Rice, water stress (photo) 90 photos 83% in 5-fold cross-validation Uses a colour-based model, which beat the deep network (76%) on this small set. Mild stress is the hardest class.
        Rice, water stress (thermal) 100 thermal images 47% in 5-fold cross-validation About the same as always guessing the biggest class, so it only adds a small share to the result.
        Rice, nutrient deficiency 1,156 leaf photos 81% per photo on held-out images Phosphorus is often mistaken for nitrogen. It cannot recognise a healthy leaf.
        Maize, water stress 18,040 small images 73% on a separate test set Healthy and severe stress are found well. Mild stress is often confused with the others.
        Maize, nutrient deficiency 463 leaf photos 83% per photo on held-out images Potassium has the fewest images and is the least reliable class.
